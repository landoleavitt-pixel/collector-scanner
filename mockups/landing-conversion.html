Brand & conversion review · Fields & Floors · 21 Oct 2026

The look is right. The first ten seconds are not.

Fields & Floors already looks like a premium collector brand. Very few hobby tools do. The problem is what happens after someone lands: they wait through an intro, guess what the product is, and then face a form before they see a single card. Everything below is about getting a new visitor to a real result in one tap, and then asking for the account at the moment they actually want one.

Keep

The brand is an asset

  • Dark, warm, gold palette reads like an auction house, not a coupon site.
  • The live featured card in the hero is the best thing on the page.
  • The rarity colours (gold, silver, copper) teach rarity at a glance.
  • "Pick up where you left off" for returning visitors.
Fix

Where visitors drop off

  • About two seconds of splash animation on every visit.
  • The headline sounds nice but doesn't say what the site is.
  • Tapping a suggested name opens a filter form instead of results.
  • We ask for an account before we've shown any value.
  • No proof that it works, and no tracking of where people quit.

01 · The biggest leak

A new visitor should see cards after one tap.

Today, typing a name or tapping "Paul Skenes" opens Step 02 · Refine: a long page of switches for autograph, rookie, company, listing type, condition and price. A first-time visitor doesn't know what they want yet. They want to see what the site does. Every extra screen before the first result loses people.

Do this
  • Search runs immediately with sensible defaults: numbered cards, rarest first.
  • The filters already live in the results sidebar and the phone filter drawer. Let people adjust there.
  • Keep the Refine screen only for editing a saved search.
  • Turn the name chips into ready-made hunts: "Caitlin Clark · autos numbered to 25 or less".

02 + 03 · The hero

Say what it is, then get out of the way.

"Find the one." is a good brand line, but a stranger can't tell from it whether this is a shop, a price guide or a grading service. The plain explanation sits in small grey text underneath. Lead with what it does and keep "Find the one" as the small line above it.

The splash intro is beautiful the first time. By the third visit it's a two-second wait. On the phone test, the hero was still blank four seconds after the page started loading.

Do this
  • Headline: "Every numbered card on eBay, on one page."
  • Play the splash on the first visit only, and cut it to under a second.
  • Swap the generic name chips for three ready-made hunts.
  • Add a line of reassurance under the search: free, no account, real eBay listings.
  • Add a strip of live numbers under the hero (see 05).

03b · Phones

On a phone, the search box should be visible without scrolling.

Most hobby traffic comes from phones, and much of it from links shared in Discord, X and Facebook groups. On a phone the featured card comes first and the search box sits near the bottom of the screen. On smaller iPhones it falls below the edge of the screen.

Do this
  • Phones: headline, search, then ready-made hunts. The card moves into a smaller "Live right now" row.
  • Keep the big floating card on desktop, where there's room for it.
  • Clean up recent searches: proper capitals, no near-duplicates ("Paul Skenes" and "Paul Skeenes").

04 · Asking for the account

Ask for the sign-up at the moment they want it.

The "Create free account" band sits right under the hero, before the visitor has seen a single card. It's asking for a commitment before we've earned one. The two moments where people actually want an account are when they star a card they like, and when a search has nothing new and they want to be told when something lists.

Do this
  • Starring a card while signed out opens a small popup: "Keep this card", with Apple and Google sign-in.
  • Under results: "Get an email when a new one lists." This is where the alert plan sells itself.
  • Move the free-account band below "How it works", or drop it.
  • Rename step 3 from "Upgrade to be alerted" to "Get an email when it lists". Lead with the benefit, not the price.

On trust: one real quote from a collector, plus a live count of listings checked, beats any amount of design polish. Ask your first ten active users for a sentence each. Only show numbers we actually measure.

06b · Plans

Three plans that each sell something.

The "Anonymous" column lists two features and a button that reloads the page. It takes a third of the space and sells nothing. Each column also repeats the full list, so the difference between plans is hard to spot.

This matches the layout you picked on 20 Oct (Base raised in the middle and shown first on phones, the annual price, and 1 free alert). It just hasn't been built yet.

Do this
  • Free · Base · Premium (coming soon), with Base raised and marked "Most popular".
  • Each column lists only what it adds: "Everything in Free, plus…"
  • Show the yearly price as a saving: "$59/yr, about 2 months free".

07 · Measure it

You can't improve what you can't see.

The site counts page views but nothing after that. We can't see how many visitors search, open a card, or start signing up. Track these six moments, then judge every change above by whether the numbers move. Vercel's custom events may need a paid plan; logging the same events to Supabase is free. That's a cost call for Landon.

Rollout

Three weeks, smallest changes first.

Week 1
  • Turn on tracking (07) so there's a before-and-after.
  • Splash on the first visit only (02).
  • New headline, header and copy edits (03, 06).
Week 2
  • Search goes straight to results; ready-made hunts replace name chips (01).
  • Phone hero reorder (03b).
Week 3
  • Sign-up popup on star, alert band under results (04).
  • Plans rebuild (06b) and proof strip with real numbers (05).
