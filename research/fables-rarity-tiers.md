# One simple rarity grouping for Foils & Fables

*Research only, 2026-11-01. No code changed. Every number below links to the page it came from. Where I couldn't back something up, it is marked **UNSOURCED** and listed again at the end.*

## The short version

**Five tiers, the same words in every game:**

| Tier | Plain meaning | How often a pack has one |
|---|---|---|
| **Common** | You get several in every pack | Several per pack |
| **Uncommon** | You get a few in most packs | A few per pack |
| **Rare** | Most packs have one | About 1 in 1 to 1 in 3 packs |
| **Very rare** | A few per box of packs | About 1 in 4 to 1 in 40 packs |
| **Extremely rare** | Less than one per box | Rarer than 1 in 40 packs, often 1 in 100 or more |

(A "box" here means a booster box of about 24 to 36 packs.)

**What the evidence says:**
- **No publisher publishes pull rates** for Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece or Lorcana (the sources say so directly). Wizards publishes odds for some Magic products. Everything else is community counting, and different counts often disagree. So the tiers are a **best reading of the evidence**, not official facts, and the copy should say "how often a card turns up in packs, from community pack-opening counts".
- **Printed names mislead often.** "Super Rare" in Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece and Lorcana is found in roughly every 2nd or 3rd pack. Lorcana's "Legendary" turns up in roughly 1 pack in 5, while "Enchanted" is about 1 in 100. Pokémon's "Hyper Rare" is about twice as rare per pack as a "Special Illustration Rare", but each individual card of the two is about equally hard to find. Details below.
- **Chance of the *kind* of card is not chance of one *specific* card.** If a set has 10 cards of a rarity, any one of them is about 10 times rarer than the rarity as a whole. The tiers use the first (the rarity as a whole). A worked example for the specific-card view is in section 4.

## 3. Where the printed names mislead

| Game | The name suggests | What the numbers say |
|---|---|---|
| Pokémon | **Hyper Rare** is above **Special Illustration Rare** | HR is about 1 in 180 packs; SIR is about 1 in 45–100. HR is about 2 times rarer per pack, **but** a set has more SIRs than HRs, so any one specific HR and any one specific SIR end up about equally hard to find (section 4). Both go in *Extremely rare*. |
| Pokémon | **Ultra Rare** is above **Illustration Rare** | UR 1 in 12, IR 1 in 9: nearly the same. Both *Very rare*. |
| Pokémon | **Double Rare** is near the bottom | About 1 in 5 packs, so about 7 per box. Fairly easy; it sits at the bottom of *Very rare*. |
| Pokémon | Prismatic Evolutions is like other sets | Its SIR odds (1 in 45) are about twice as good as Surging Sparks (1 in 87). Odds vary per set, so any tier is approximate. |
| Yu-Gi-Oh! | **Super Rare** | Found in about 3 packs in 4 of the top slot. Not very rare at all. |
| Yu-Gi-Oh! | **Secret Rare** and **Ultra Rare** | Secret is about 1 in 12 and Ultra about 1 in 6, even though "Ultra" sounds bigger. Secret's price comes from its being the set's chase card. |
| One Piece | **Super Rare** | About 1 pack in 3. Quite common. |
| One Piece | **Secret Rare** | About 1 per box, but a **Manga Rare** is at least about 12 times rarer. Both are "rare" by name. |
| Lorcana | **Super Rare** | About half of all packs. |
| Lorcana | **Legendary** (top-sounding) vs **Enchanted** | Legendary about 1 in 5 packs; Enchanted about 1 in 100. Enchanted is 20 times rarer. |
| Magic | **Mythic Rare** | About 1 pack in 7, so about 5 per box. Mythic with a special treatment can be 20 times rarer or more. |
| All | "Rare" | In every game, "Rare" is the card most packs contain. |

## 4. Specific card versus the whole rarity (worked example)

For any one chase card, the real odds are roughly **(odds of the rarity) × (number of cards of that rarity in the set)**. Using the Mega Evolution base set: community odds from [Card Codex](https://cardcodex.com/blog/mega-evolution-base-set-pull-rates/) and the number of cards of each rarity from [TCGdex](https://api.tcgdex.net/v2/en/cards) (counted 2026-11-01):

| Rarity | Cards in the set | Odds of the rarity | A specific card, roughly |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Double Rare | 10 | 1 in 5 | 1 in 50 packs |
| Illustration Rare | 22 | 1 in 9 | 1 in 200 |
| Ultra Rare | 22 | 1 in 12 | 1 in 260 |
| Special Illustration Rare | 10 | 1 in 101 | 1 in 1,000 |
| Mega Hyper Rare | 2 | 1 in 1,260 | 1 in 2,500 (matches the 1 in 2,520 Card Codex gives for a specific card) |

Same method, other sets: Surging Sparks has 11 SIRs (1 in 87 → about 1 in 960 per card) and 6 Hyper Rares (1 in 189 → about 1 in 1,130 per card). That's why "Hyper Rare is rarer than SIR" is true for the rarity as a whole but not for a single card.

**Why it matters for the tiers:** I kept the tiers on the rarity as a whole because that's what a listing title says and it works the same for every card. But a specific Double Rare (1 in 50) is rarer than the tier name "Very rare" suggests. If we want to show it later, the card-family catalogs already hold the per-set card counts. This is a **derived estimate** built from community odds, so it should be labelled as an estimate.

## 5. What I can't back with a source

- **No official pull rates** exist for Pokémon, Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece or Lorcana. Everything is community counting, often from small samples (about 200–300 packs for Lorcana, about 240 for the Yu-Gi-Oh! set I checked, none stated for One Piece).
- **Conflicts between sources:** Mega Hyper Rare (1 in 1,260 versus 1 in 80–200), Yu-Gi-Oh! Super Rares per box (7–8 versus 18), One Piece Secret Rares (1 versus 2 per box), Manga Rare (1 in 288 to 1 in 1,728), Lorcana Legendary (19% versus 28%).
- **No source at all:** Lorcana Epic; One Piece Treasure Rare, Leader rate and prize-card counts; Pokémon Mega Attack Rare, Black White Rare, Shiny Rare, Radiant Rare, Gold Star, Shining and Crystal; Yu-Gi-Oh! Ghost, Ultimate, Collector's, Platinum and Quarter Century per-pack rates, and the pack layout for plain Rares; Magic serialized pack odds; Japanese-pack odds for any game; 1st Edition and Shadowless print runs; the supply of vintage Magic and Pokémon.
- **Official Magic odds:** TCGZen cites Wizards' "Nuts & Bolts #16" for 85.7% / 14.3%. I opened that Wizards page but it didn't show the odds, so I'm treating it as "reported" until someone checks.
- **Pages I could not open:** PokeBeach (access denied), Deltias Gaming (error) and CardShopLive (rate-limited). I did not try to get around any of these, so those figures come from search summaries or other sites quoting them.
- **Odds vary by set.** The tiers use typical figures. A few sets (Prismatic Evolutions, special reprint sets, premium products) run very different odds.

## 6. Notes for the engine and screens

- **Names:** today's tier names in `lib/tcg/ladders.js` (Grail, Relic, Treasure, Charm) are placeholders marked as awaiting approval, and they fail the grandparent test. Five plain tiers replace them. The existing colour slots would carry over as: Extremely rare = Grail colour, Very rare = Ultra, Rare = Rare, Common and Uncommon = plain (no colour). The old fourth coloured slot ("Scarce") folds into Rare.
- **Show both names:** the chip says the tier ("Extremely rare") and the game's own name small next to it ("Special Illustration Rare"), so collectors keep the words they know.
- **Sorting:** tier first, then the game's own order inside the tier (rarest first by printed rarity), then the serial number if there is one. That keeps today's behaviour inside each tier.
- **Copy:** "How often this kind of card turns up in packs, from community pack-opening counts. Publishers don't publish exact odds." Never "1 in X" on a card.
- **Not in packs:** promos, prize cards and event cards don't have pull rates. Show the tier only if a source backs it; otherwise show them with a "Promo" label and no tier.
